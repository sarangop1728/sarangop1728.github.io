Chapter 8The Mordell–Weil theorem

This chapter follows [Sil09, Sections VIII.1–VIII.3 and VIII.9], with the point of view of [Poo02, Sections 8–10]. The counting of elements of bounded height follows [HS00, Sections B.4–B.6].

The Mordell–Weil theorem says that the group \(E(k)\) of rational points of an elliptic curve over a number field is finitely generated. Its proof has two steps of a very different nature. The first, the weak Mordell–Weil theorem, says that \(E(k)/mE(k)\) is finite for every \(m \geq 2\), and the second step needs it for a single \(m\). It is an algebraic statement, and it is proved with the Galois cohomology of Chapter 7 and the local fields of Chapter 6. The second is a theory of heights. This chapter starts with the first step, first in a case where everything is explicit.

Situation 8.0.1.

  • \(k\) is a number field, with set of places \(\Places_k\) and completions \(k_v\). For a non-archimedean place \(v\) we use the notation of Situations 6.0.2 and 6.4.1: the ring of integers \(\calO_v\), the residue field \(\kappa(v)\) of characteristic \(p\), and the inertia group \(I_v\).
  • Since \(\operatorname{char} k = 0\), separable closures are algebraic closures. We write \(\kbar\) and \(\overline{k_v}\) for the \(\ksep\) and \(k_v^{\operatorname{sep}}\) of Chapters 6 and 7, and \(\Gal_k \coloneqq \Gal(\kbar/k)\). For an extension \(F\) of \(k\) inside \(\kbar\), \(\Gal_F \coloneqq \Gal(\kbar/F)\).
  • Elements of Galois groups are \(\sigma, \tau, \gamma\). For a \(\kbar\)-point \(Q\) of a variety over \(k\), \(\sigma(Q)\) is obtained by applying \(\sigma\) to the coordinates of \(Q\).
  • As in Situation 7.8.1, for each \(v \in \Places_k\) we fix a \(k\)-embedding \(\kbar \to \overline{k_v}\). Restriction to \(\kbar\) then identifies \(\Gal_{k_v}\) with a decomposition group in \(\Gal_k\), and \(I_v\) with a subgroup of it.
  • \(E\) is an elliptic curve over \(k\), and \(m \geq 2\) is an integer. For an abelian group \(B\), \(B/m \coloneqq B/mB\) and \(B[m]\) is its \(m\)-torsion subgroup.
  • For a non-archimedean place \(v\), \(E\) has good (respectively bad) reduction at \(v\) if \(E_{k_v}\) has good (respectively bad) reduction (Definition 6.3.1).

Extensions of \(k\) are compared with inertia in the same way as cohomology classes in Section 7.8.

Definition 8.0.2. Let \(F\) be a Galois extension of \(k\) inside \(\kbar\), possibly infinite, and let \(v\) be a non-archimedean place of \(k\). Then \(F\) is unramified at \(v\) if \(I_v \subseteq \Gal_F\), that is, if \(I_v\) fixes \(F\). For a finite set \(T \subseteq \Places_k\), \(F\) is unramified outside \(T\) if it is unramified at every non-archimedean \(v \notin T\).

Another embedding \(\kbar \to \overline{k_v}\) replaces \(I_v\) by a conjugate \(\gamma I_v\gamma^{-1}\), with \(\gamma \in \Gal_k\), and \(\Gal_F\) is normal, so the condition does not depend on the embedding. A compositum of Galois extensions unramified at \(v\) is unramified at \(v\), since \(\Gal_{FF'} = \Gal_F \cap \Gal_{F'}\), and so is a Galois subextension \(F'' \subseteq F\), since \(\Gal_F \subseteq \Gal_{F''}\). Let \(\chi\colon \Gal_k \to A\) be a continuous homomorphism to a finite abelian group, a class of \(H^1(k, A)\) for the trivial action (Proposition 7.2.3). The fixed field of \(\ker\chi\) is unramified at \(v\) exactly when \(\chi\) is trivial on \(I_v\), that is, when the class \(\chi\) is unramified at \(v\) in the sense of Section 7.8. For a quadratic field this is the familiar criterion: if \(p\) is an odd prime and \(d \in \Q^\times\), then \(\Q(\sqrt d)\) is unramified at \(p\) if and only if \(v_p(d)\) is even, by Proposition 7.7.4 applied to the class \(\delta(d)\colon \sigma \mapsto \sigma(\sqrt d)/\sqrt d\).

Draft14 Oct 20268.1 The case \(m = 2\) over \(\Q\) with rational \(2\)-torsion

The proof of the weak Mordell–Weil theorem is easier to see in a special case, where the class group and the unit theorem disappear and the local input is elementary. This section follows [Sil09, Section VIII.1] for \(m = 2\) and \(k = \Q\).

Situation 8.1.1. In addition to Situation 8.0.1:

  • \(k = \Q\) and \(m = 2\). For a prime \(p\), \(I_p \subseteq \Gal_{\Q_p} \subseteq \Gal_\Q\) is the inertia group at \(p\).

  • \(E\) is given by

    \begin{equation*} y^2 = (x - e_1)(x - e_2)(x - e_3) \end{equation*}

    with distinct \(e_1, e_2, e_3 \in \Z\). Its points of order two are \(T_i \coloneqq (e_i, 0)\), so \(E[2](\Qbar) = \{O, T_1, T_2, T_3\} \subseteq E(\Q)\).

  • \(S \coloneqq \{\infty, 2\} \cup \{p : p \mid (e_1 - e_2)(e_1 - e_3)(e_2 - e_3)\}\), a finite set of places of \(\Q\).
  • \(L\) is the compositum inside \(\Qbar\) of the fields \(\Q(Q)\), for the \(\Qbar\)-points \(Q\) of \(E\) with \(2Q \in E(\Q)\). Silverman [Sil09] writes \(L = \Q([2]^{-1}E(\Q))\).

The group law of \(E\) and the map \([2]\) are given by rational functions with coefficients in \(\Q\) (Algorithm 4.2.7), so every \(\sigma \in \Gal_\Q\) acts on \(E(\Qbar)\) by group automorphisms that commute with \([2]\).

Definition 8.1.2. Let \(P \in E(\Q)\) and \(\sigma \in \Gal_\Q\), and choose a \(\Qbar\)-point \(Q\) of \(E\) with \(2Q = P\). The Kummer pairing of \(E\) is the map

\begin{equation*} \kappa\colon E(\Q) \times \Gal_\Q \to E[2](\Qbar), \qquad \kappa(P, \sigma) \coloneqq \sigma(Q) - Q. \end{equation*}

Such a \(\Qbar\)-point \(Q\) exists, since \([2]\) is surjective on \(E(\Qbar)\) (part 1 of Proposition 7.5.1).

Proposition 8.1.3. Let \(P, P' \in E(\Q)\) and \(\sigma, \tau \in \Gal_\Q\).

  1. \(\kappa(P, \sigma)\) lies in \(E[2](\Qbar)\) and does not depend on the choice of \(Q\).
  2. \(\kappa(P + P', \sigma) = \kappa(P, \sigma) + \kappa(P', \sigma)\) and \(\kappa(P, \sigma\tau) = \kappa(P, \sigma) + \kappa(P, \tau)\).
  3. \(\kappa(P, \sigma) = O\) for every \(\sigma \in \Gal_\Q\) if and only if \(P \in 2E(\Q)\).
  4. \(\kappa(P, \sigma) = O\) for every \(P \in E(\Q)\) if and only if \(\sigma \in \Gal_L\).

  5. \(L/\Q\) is Galois, \(\Gal(L/\Q)\) is abelian of exponent dividing \(2\), and \(\kappa\) induces a pairing

    \begin{equation*} E(\Q)/2 \times \Gal(L/\Q) \to E[2](\Qbar) \end{equation*}

    for which \(E(\Q)/2 \to \Hom_{\mathrm{conts}}(\Gal(L/\Q), E[2](\Qbar))\) and \(\Gal(L/\Q) \to \Hom(E(\Q)/2, E[2](\Qbar))\) are injective. In particular \(E(\Q)/2\) is finite if and only if \(L/\Q\) is finite.

Proof.

For part 1, \(2(\sigma(Q) - Q) = \sigma(2Q) - 2Q = \sigma(P) - P = O\), as \(P\) is a rational point. Another choice of \(Q\) is \(Q + T\) with \(T \in E[2](\Qbar)\), and \(\sigma(T) = T\) because \(T\) is a rational point. So \(\sigma(Q + T) - (Q + T) = \sigma(Q) - Q\).

For part 2, if \(2Q = P\) and \(2Q' = P'\), then \(2(Q + Q') = P + P'\), and \(\sigma(Q + Q') - (Q + Q') = (\sigma(Q) - Q) + (\sigma(Q') - Q')\). Next,

\begin{equation*} (\sigma\tau)(Q) - Q = \bigl(\sigma(Q) - Q\bigr) + \sigma\bigl(\tau(Q) - Q\bigr) = \kappa(P, \sigma) + \sigma\bigl(\kappa(P, \tau)\bigr), \end{equation*}

and \(\kappa(P, \tau)\) is a point of order dividing two, hence rational, hence fixed by \(\sigma\). This and part 1 are the two places where we use that \(E[2](\Qbar) \subseteq E(\Q)\).

For part 3, if \(P = 2Q\) with \(Q \in E(\Q)\), then \(\sigma(Q) = Q\) for every \(\sigma\). Conversely, if \(\sigma(Q) = Q\) for every \(\sigma\), then the coordinates of \(Q\) lie in \(\Qbar^{\Gal_\Q} = \Q\) (Proposition 7.1.1), so \(P = 2Q \in 2E(\Q)\).

For part 4, if \(\sigma \in \Gal_L\) and \(2Q \in E(\Q)\), then the coordinates of \(Q\) lie in \(L\), so \(\sigma(Q) = Q\). Conversely, if \(\kappa(\cdot, \sigma) = O\), then \(\sigma\) fixes every \(\Qbar\)-point \(Q\) with \(2Q \in E(\Q)\), hence every field \(\Q(Q)\), hence their compositum \(L\).

For part 5, by part 2 the map \(\sigma \mapsto \kappa(\cdot, \sigma)\) is a homomorphism from \(\Gal_\Q\) to the abelian group \(\Hom(E(\Q), E[2](\Qbar))\), which is killed by \(2\). Its kernel is \(\Gal_L\) by part 4. So \(\Gal_L\) is normal, \(L/\Q\) is Galois by Proposition 7.1.1, and \(\Gal(L/\Q) \cong \Gal_\Q/\Gal_L\) embeds in \(\Hom(E(\Q), E[2](\Qbar))\). It is therefore abelian of exponent dividing \(2\). The pairing factors through \(E(\Q)/2\) and \(\Gal(L/\Q)\), and parts 3 and 4 say that the two induced maps are injective. Each \(\kappa(P, \cdot)\) is continuous, being constant on the cosets of \(\Gal_{\Q(Q)}\). If \(E(\Q)/2\) is finite, \(\Gal(L/\Q)\) embeds in a finite group, and if \(L/\Q\) is finite, \(E(\Q)/2\) does.

In the language of Chapter 7, \(\kappa(P, \cdot)\) is the cocycle of Proposition 7.5.1 representing the Kummer class \(\delta(P)\). As \(\Gal_\Q\) acts trivially on \(E[2](\Qbar)\), cocycles are homomorphisms (Proposition 7.2.3), and part 3 is the injectivity of \(\delta\) on \(E(\Q)/2\).

Remark 8.1.4. Part 5 of Proposition 8.1.3 turns the finiteness of \(E(\Q)/2\) into the finiteness of the field \(L\). This should be surprising. The same construction for the multiplicative group, with \(\sqrt a\) in place of a half of \(P\), is the pairing

\begin{equation*} \Q^\times/\Q^{\times 2} \times \Gal_\Q \to \mu_2(\Qbar), \qquad (a, \sigma) \mapsto \sigma(\sqrt a)/\sqrt a, \end{equation*}

which is perfect in the same sense (part 1 of Corollary 7.4.7, and [Sil09, Remark VIII.1.2.1]). There the analogue of \(L\) is \(\Q(\sqrt a : a \in \Q^\times)\), the compositum of all quadratic fields, which is an infinite extension of \(\Q\): the classes of \(-1\) and of the primes are independent in \(\Q^\times/\Q^{\times 2}\), by unique factorization. For \(E\), by contrast, \(E(\Q)\) may be infinite, and the coordinates of the multiples of a rational point of infinite order involve more and more primes (Example 4.2.8). One would expect the halves of these rational points to generate more and more quadratic fields, as \(\sqrt p\) does for each prime \(p\). They do not, and Proposition 8.1.5 explains why.

Proposition 8.1.5. Let \(p \notin S\) be a prime, \(Q \in E(\Qbar)\) a \(\Qbar\)-point with \(2Q \in E(\Q)\), and \(\tau \in I_p\). Then \(\tau(Q) = Q\). Hence \(L/\Q\) is unramified outside \(S\).

Proof.

Through the embedding \(\Qbar \to \overline{\Q_p}\), \(Q\) is a \(\overline{\Q_p}\)-point of \(E\). Let \(K'\) be the finite Galois extension of \(\Q_p\) generated by the coordinates of \(Q\) and their conjugates, with ring of integers \(\calO'\) and residue field \(\kappa'\). The discriminant of the equation of \(E\) is

\begin{equation*} \Delta = 16(e_1 - e_2)^2(e_1 - e_3)^2(e_2 - e_3)^2, \end{equation*}

and \(p \nmid \Delta\) because \(p \notin S\). So the equation is minimal at \(p\) and \(E\) has good reduction at \(p\) (part 1 of Proposition 6.3.2). Let \(E_p\) be its reduction modulo \(p\), the \(E_\pi\) of Definition 6.2.4 with \(\pi = p\), an elliptic curve over \(\F_p\). By part 2 of Proposition 6.3.7, the equation is minimal for \(E_{K'}\), and the reduction of \(E_{K'}\) is the elliptic curve \((E_p)_{\kappa'}\). By Proposition 6.2.8 over \(K'\), reduction \(R \mapsto \bar R\) is a homomorphism \(E(K') \to E_p(\kappa')\).

The element \(\tau\) maps \(K'\) to itself and preserves the valuation, so it maps \(\calO'\) to itself, and \(\overline{\tau(R)} = \bar\tau(\bar R)\), where \(\bar\tau\) is the action of \(\tau\) on \(\kappa'\). As \(\tau \in I_p\), \(\bar\tau\) is the identity, so

\begin{equation*} \overline{\tau(Q) - Q} = \bar\tau(\bar Q) - \bar Q = \bar O. \end{equation*}

By part 1 of Proposition 8.1.3, \(\tau(Q) - Q \in E[2](\Qbar) = \{O, T_1, T_2, T_3\}\). The reductions \(\bar T_i = (\bar e_i, 0)\) are affine, so they differ from \(\bar O = (0 : 1 : 0)\), and they are distinct because \(p \nmid e_i - e_j\) for \(i \neq j\). So reduction is injective on \(E[2](\Qbar)\), and \(\tau(Q) = Q\). Thus \(I_p\) fixes each field \(\Q(Q)\), hence their compositum \(L\).

Remark 8.1.6. Let us see where the same argument fails for the multiplicative group. Let \(p\) be an odd prime, \(a \in \Q^\times\), \(\alpha \coloneqq \sqrt a\) and \(\tau \in I_p\). Then \(\tau(\alpha)/\alpha \in \mu_2(\Qbar)\), and \(\mu_2(\Qbar) = \{\pm 1\}\) injects into \(\F_p^\times\) under reduction. If \(v_p(a) = 0\), then \(\alpha\) is a unit, it reduces to an element of \(\Fbar_p^\times\), the reduction of \(\tau(\alpha)/\alpha\) is \(1\), and \(\tau\) fixes \(\alpha\). But if \(v_p(a)\) is odd, \(\alpha\) reduces to \(0\), which is not in \(\Gm(\Fbar_p)\). The argument has nothing to say, and indeed \(\Q_p(\sqrt p)\) is ramified (part 3 of Example 6.4.5). For \(E\), every \(\overline{\Q_p}\)-point reduces to a point of \(E_p(\Fbar_p)\), because \(E\) is projective. No rational point of \(E\) escapes the group under reduction, as \(p\) escapes \(\Gm\). We return to this in Remark 8.3.5.

The proposition has an elementary shadow. Let \(P = (x_0, y_0) \in E(\Q)\), and let \(p \notin S\). If \(v_p(x_0) < 0\), then \(2v_p(y_0) = 3v_p(x_0)\), so \(v_p(x_0 - e_i) = v_p(x_0)\) is even. If \(v_p(x_0) \geq 0\), then at most one of the three factors \(x_0 - e_i\) is divisible by \(p\), as their differences \(e_j - e_i\) are prime to \(p\). That factor has the valuation of \(y_0^2\), which is even. So in all cases \(v_p(x_0 - e_i)\) is even when \(x_0 \neq e_i\): the class of \(x_0 - e_i\) in \(\Q^\times/\Q^{\times 2}\) lies in the finite group \(\Q(S, 2)\) of Example 7.8.5, however large the numerator and denominator of \(x_0\). The map \(P \mapsto (x_0 - e_1, x_0 - e_2)\), corrected at \(T_1\) and \(T_2\), is an explicit form of the Kummer map \(E(\Q)/2 \to \Q(S, 2)^2\) [Sil09, Proposition X.1.4], and it is the starting point of the descent in Chapter 9.

Theorem 8.1.7. In Situation 8.1.1, \([L : \Q] \leq 2^{\#S}\) and \(\#E(\Q)/2E(\Q) \leq 4^{\#S}\).

Proof.

We do not know yet that \(L\) is finite. Let \(F \subseteq L\) be a finite extension of \(\Q\). As \(\Gal(L/\Q)\) is abelian of exponent dividing \(2\) (part 5 of Proposition 8.1.3), \(F/\Q\) is Galois, abelian of exponent dividing \(2\). Since \(\mu_2(\Qbar) \subseteq \Q\), part 2 of Corollary 7.4.7 gives \(F = \Q(\Delta^{1/2})\) for a subgroup \(\Q^{\times 2} \subseteq \Delta \subseteq \Q^\times\) with \([F : \Q] = (\Delta : \Q^{\times 2})\). For \(d \in \Delta\), the field \(\Q(\sqrt d) \subseteq L\) is unramified outside \(S\) by Proposition 8.1.5, so the class \(\delta(d)\) of Corollary 7.4.6 lies in \(H^1(\Q, \mu_2; S)\), and the class of \(d\) lies in \(\Q(S, 2)\) by part 1 of Proposition 7.8.3. So \(\Delta/\Q^{\times 2} \subseteq \Q(S, 2)\), a group of order \(2^{\#S}\) (Example 7.8.5), and \([F : \Q] \leq 2^{\#S}\).

Choose such an \(F_0\) of maximal degree. For any other \(F\), the compositum \(F_0F\) is a finite extension inside \(L\), of degree at most \([F_0 : \Q]\), so \(F \subseteq F_0\). As \(L\) is the union of its finite subextensions, \(L = F_0\) and \([L : \Q] \leq 2^{\#S}\). So \(\Gal(L/\Q) \cong (\Z/2\Z)^t\) with \(t \leq \#S\), and by part 5 of Proposition 8.1.3,

\begin{equation*} \#E(\Q)/2E(\Q) \leq \#\Hom\bigl((\Z/2\Z)^t, (\Z/2\Z)^2\bigr) = 4^t \leq 4^{\#S}. \end{equation*}

Granting the Mordell–Weil theorem, \(E(\Q) \cong \Z^r \times E(\Q)_{\mathrm{tors}}\) for some \(r \geq 0\), and \(E(\Q)_{\mathrm{tors}}/2\) has the order of \(E(\Q)_{\mathrm{tors}}[2] = E[2](\Qbar)\), which is \(4\). So \(\#E(\Q)/2E(\Q) = 2^{r + 2}\), and Theorem 8.1.7 bounds the rank: \(r \leq 2\#S - 2\).

Example 8.1.8. Let \(E\colon y^2 = x^3 - x\), with \((e_1, e_2, e_3) = (0, 1, -1)\). The differences \(e_i - e_j\) are \(\pm 1\) and \(\pm 2\), so \(S = \{\infty, 2\}\), \(\Q(S, 2) = \langle -1, 2 \rangle\), \(L \subseteq \Q(i, \sqrt 2)\), and \(\#E(\Q)/2E(\Q) \leq 16\). The halves of \(T_1 = (0, 0)\) are \((i, \pm(1 - i))\) and \((-i, \pm(1 + i))\), defined over \(\Q(i)\). The halves of \(T_2\) and \(T_3\) generate \(\Q(\sqrt 2)\) and \(\Q(\sqrt 2, \sqrt{-2})\). So \(L = \Q(i, \sqrt 2) = \Q(\zeta_8)\), ramified only at \(2\), and the bound on \([L : \Q]\) is attained. Here \(E(\Q) = E[2](\Qbar)\), so \(\#E(\Q)/2E(\Q) = 4\), and only \(4\) of the \(16\) homomorphisms \(\Gal(L/\Q) \to E[2](\Qbar)\) come from rational points.

Example 8.1.9. Let \(E\colon y^2 = x^3 - 25x\), with \((e_1, e_2, e_3) = (0, 5, -5)\). The differences \(e_i - e_j\) are \(\pm 5\) and \(\pm 10\), so \(S = \{\infty, 2, 5\}\), \(\Q(S, 2) = \langle -1, 2, 5 \rangle\) and \(L \subseteq \Q(i, \sqrt 2, \sqrt 5)\). Magma finds that \(E(\Q)\) is generated by \(T_1\), \(T_2\) and \(P \coloneqq (-4, 6)\), of infinite order, so \(\#E(\Q)/2E(\Q) = 8\), against the bound \(64\). The multiples of \(P\) have

\begin{gather*} x(2P) = \Bigl(\frac{41}{12}\Bigr)^2, \qquad x(3P) = -\frac{2439844}{5094049} = -\Bigl(\frac{2 \cdot 11 \cdot 71}{37 \cdot 61}\Bigr)^2, \\ x(4P) = \frac{11183412793921}{2234116132416} = \Bigl(\frac{3344161}{2^3 \cdot 3 \cdot 7^2 \cdot 31 \cdot 41}\Bigr)^2, \end{gather*}

and \(x(3P) - 5 = -(3^2 \cdot 587/2257)^2\), \(x(3P) + 5 = (4799/2257)^2\). The primes in these coordinates grow, but each appears to an even power, as Remark 8.1.6 predicts. So the classes of \(x(nP) - e_i\) never leave \(\langle -1, 2, 5 \rangle\). For instance, \(Q \coloneqq (2 + i, 7i - 1)\) satisfies \(2Q = P\), and the halves of \(3P\) are the \(Q + P + T\) with \(T \in E[2](\Qbar)\), all defined over \(\Q(i)\). Magma also finds that the halves of \(T_1\) generate \(\Q(i, \sqrt 5)\), those of \(T_2\) generate \(\Q(\sqrt 2, \sqrt 5)\), and \(L = \Q(i, \sqrt 2, \sqrt 5)\), of degree \(8 = 2^{\#S}\).

We are in Situation 8.0.1. This section is [Sil09, Section VIII.1]: the argument of Section 8.1 for any \(m\) and any number field.

Theorem 8.2.1 (Weak Mordell–Weil). The group \(E(k)/mE(k)\) is finite.

We call Theorem 8.2.1 the weak Mordell--Weil theorem. The first step reduces it to the case \(E[m](\kbar) \subseteq E(k)\), which made the Kummer pairing bilinear in Section 8.1.

Lemma 8.2.2. Let \(K\) be a finite Galois extension of \(k\) inside \(\kbar\). If \(E(K)/mE(K)\) is finite, then \(E(k)/mE(k)\) is finite.

Proof.

Let \(\Phi\) be the kernel of \(E(k)/m \to E(K)/m\), so that \(0 \to \Phi \to E(k)/m \to E(K)/m\) is exact, and it suffices to show that \(\Phi\) is finite. Let \(\delta_k\colon E(k)/m \to H^1(k, E[m])\) and \(\delta_K\colon E(K)/m \to H^1(K, E[m])\) be the Kummer maps, injective by Proposition 7.5.1. For \(P \in E(k)\), the cocycle \(\sigma \mapsto \sigma(Q) - Q\) restricted to \(\Gal_K\) represents \(\delta_K(P)\), so \(\Res \circ \delta_k = \delta_K\) on \(E(k)/m\). Hence \(\delta_k\) maps \(\Phi\) into the kernel of \(\Res\colon H^1(k, E[m]) \to H^1(K, E[m])\). By Proposition 7.3.5, with \(G = \Gal_k\) and \(H = \Gal_K\), this kernel is the image of \(H^1(\Gal(K/k), E[m](K))\). The cocycles defining this group are maps between the finite sets \(\Gal(K/k)\) and \(E[m](K)\), so it is finite, and so is \(\Phi\) [Sil09, Lemma VIII.1.1.1 and Section VIII.2].

The field \(K \coloneqq k(E[m])\) is a finite Galois extension of \(k\), a number field, with \(E[m](\kbar) \subseteq E(K)\) (the discussion after Example 7.1.3). By Lemma 8.2.2, it suffices to prove Theorem 8.2.1 when \(E[m](\kbar) \subseteq E(k)\). Then Definition 8.1.2 generalizes word for word. For \(P \in E(k)\) and \(\sigma \in \Gal_k\), choose a \(\kbar\)-point \(Q\) with \(mQ = P\), and let

\begin{equation*} \kappa\colon E(k) \times \Gal_k \to E[m](\kbar), \qquad \kappa(P, \sigma) \coloneqq \sigma(Q) - Q, \end{equation*}

and let \(L \coloneqq k([m]^{-1}E(k))\) be the compositum inside \(\kbar\) of the fields \(k(Q)\), for the \(\kbar\)-points \(Q\) of \(E\) with \(mQ \in E(k)\).

Proposition 8.2.3. Suppose that \(E[m](\kbar) \subseteq E(k)\). Then \(\kappa\) is well defined and bilinear, its left kernel is \(mE(k)\), and its right kernel is \(\Gal_L\). So \(L/k\) is Galois, \(\Gal(L/k)\) is abelian of exponent dividing \(m\), and \(\kappa\) induces a pairing

\begin{equation*} E(k)/m \times \Gal(L/k) \to E[m](\kbar) \end{equation*}

for which both induced maps are injective. In particular \(E(k)/m\) is finite if and only if \(L/k\) is finite.

Proof.

The proof of Proposition 8.1.3 applies with \(2\) replaced by \(m\) and \(\Q\) by \(k\) [Sil09, Proposition VIII.1.2].

Proof.

8.3.1 The Kummer image at a good place

The Kummer map exists over \(k_v\) too, by Proposition 7.5.1 over \(k_v\):

\begin{equation*} \delta_v\colon E(k_v)/m \to H^1(k_v, E[m]), \end{equation*}

and \(\operatorname{res}_v \circ \delta = \delta_v\) on \(E(k)\), with the same cocycle. At the places outside \(S\) its image is exactly the group of unramified classes of Definition 7.7.2. We first count \(E(k_v)/m\).

Proposition 8.3.2. Let \(v\) be a non-archimedean place of \(k\) with \(v(m) = 0\), and \(E'\) an elliptic curve over \(k_v\). Then \(\#E'(k_v)/mE'(k_v) = \#E'(k_v)[m]\), and both are finite.

Proof.

Fix a minimal Weierstrass equation for \(E'\). The kernel of reduction \(E'_1(k_v)\) is isomorphic to the group \(\hat E'(\mm_v)\) of the formal group of \(E'\) [Sil09, Proposition VII.2.2]. Since \(m \in \calO_v^\times\), multiplication by \(m\) is an isomorphism of the formal group [Sil09, Proposition IV.2.3(b)], so it is bijective on \(\hat E'(\mm_v)\), and \(E'_1(k_v)\) is uniquely \(m\)-divisible. The quotient \(B \coloneqq E'(k_v)/E'_1(k_v)\) is finite, since \(E'(k_v)/E'_0(k_v)\) is finite by part 1 of Theorem 6.3.9, and \(E'_0(k_v)/E'_1(k_v) \cong (E'_\pi)^{\ns}(\kappa(v))\) is finite by Proposition 6.2.8. The snake lemma for multiplication by \(m\) on \(0 \to E'_1(k_v) \to E'(k_v) \to B \to 0\) gives an exact sequence

\begin{equation*} 0 \to E'_1(k_v)[m] \to E'(k_v)[m] \to B[m] \to E'_1(k_v)/m \to E'(k_v)/m \to B/m \to 0, \end{equation*}

whose terms \(E'_1(k_v)[m]\) and \(E'_1(k_v)/m\) are \(0\). So \(\#E'(k_v)[m] = \#B[m]\) and \(\#E'(k_v)/m = \#B/m\). For a finite abelian group \(B\), \(\#B[m] = \#B/m\), as both are the order of the kernel and of the cokernel of \(m\colon B \to B\).

Remark 8.3.3. When \(v(m) > 0\), the formal group contributes: \(E'(k_v)\) contains a subgroup of finite index isomorphic to \(\calO_v\) [Sil09, Proposition VII.6.3]. This subgroup has no \(m\)-torsion, and \(\#\calO_v/m\calO_v = q^{v(m)}\). The snake lemma for it, as in the proof of Proposition 8.3.2, gives

\begin{equation*} \#E'(k_v)/mE'(k_v) = q^{v(m)}\,\#E'(k_v)[m], \end{equation*}

which Chapter 9 needs.

Proposition 8.3.4. Let \(v\) be a non-archimedean place of \(k\) with \(v(m) = 0\), and \(E'\) an elliptic curve over \(k_v\) with good reduction. Then \(\delta_v(E'(k_v)/m) = H^1_{\unr}(k_v, E'[m])\).

Proof.

Let \(P \in E'(k_v)\) and \(Q \in E'(\overline{k_v})\) with \(mQ = P\). The restriction of \(\delta_v(P)\) to \(I_v\) is \(\tau \mapsto \tau(Q) - Q\), which is \(0\) by Lemma 8.2.5. So the image lies in \(H^1_{\unr}(k_v, E'[m])\). The map \(\delta_v\) is injective, and by Proposition 8.3.2 and part 3 of Proposition 7.7.3, applied to the finite module \(E'[m](\overline{k_v})\),

\begin{equation*} \#E'(k_v)/m = \#E'(k_v)[m] = \#E'[m](\overline{k_v})^{\Gal_{k_v}} = \#H^1_{\unr}(k_v, E'[m]). \end{equation*}

So the image is all of \(H^1_{\unr}(k_v, E'[m])\).

Remark 8.3.5. We can now say why \(E\) differs from \(\Gm\), the question of Remark 8.1.4. For \(\Gm\) the Kummer map \(\delta\colon k^\times/k^{\times m} \to H^1(k, \mu_m)\) is an isomorphism (Corollary 7.4.6), and its image is not unramified outside any finite set: if \(v(m) = 0\) and \(a \in k^\times\) has \(v(a) = 1\), then \(\delta(a)\) is ramified at \(v\) (Proposition 7.7.4).   • \(A\) is an abelian group, written additively, with elements \(P, Q, R\), and torsion subgroup \(A_\tors\). We write \(A_\R \coloneqq A \otimes_\Z \R\).

8.4.1 Heights and finite generation

Definition 8.4.2. Let \(X\) be a set. A height on \(X\) is a function \(h\colon X \to \R\). Two heights \(h\) and \(h'\) on \(X\) are equivalent, written \(h = h' + O(1)\), if \(\lvert h - h' \rvert\) is bounded on \(X\). A height \(h\) has the Northcott property if the set \(\{x \in X : h(x) \leq C\}\) is finite for every \(C \in \R\).

An inequality \(f \leq g + c\) that holds for one constant \(c\) holds for every larger one. So the constants that bound heights in this section are taken non-negative, and a lower bound is written \(-c\) with \(c \geq 0\).

The Northcott property depends only on the equivalence class: if \(\lvert h - h' \rvert \leq c\) on \(X\), with \(c \geq 0\), then \(\{h' \leq C\} \subseteq \{h \leq C + c\}\). A height with the Northcott property is bounded below. Indeed, if \(X\) is non-empty and \(x_0 \in X\), the set \(\{h \leq h(x_0)\}\) is finite and non-empty, and its minimum is a lower bound for \(h\).

Definition 8.4.3. A height \(h\) on \(A\) is quasi-quadratic if

\begin{equation} \label{eq:mw-quasi-parallelogram} h(P + Q) + h(P - Q) = 2h(P) + 2h(Q) + O(1), \tag{8.4.1} \end{equation}

that is, if the difference of the two sides is bounded on \(A \times A\). It is quadratic if the two sides are equal for all \(P, Q \in A\) (the parallelogram law). The pairing of a quadratic height \(q\) is

\begin{equation*} \langle P, Q \rangle_q \coloneqq q(P + Q) - q(P) - q(Q). \end{equation*}

Lemma 8.4.4. Let \(h\) be a quasi-quadratic height on \(A\).

  1. For every \(n \in \Z\), \(h(nP) = n^2 h(P) + O(1)\), with a bound depending on \(n\) but not on \(P\).
  2. If \(h\) is bounded below, then for every \(Q \in A\) there is a constant \(C(Q) \geq 0\) with \(h(P + Q) \leq 2h(P) + C(Q)\) for all \(P \in A\).
Proof.

Let \(c \geq 0\) bound the difference of the two sides of \eqref{eq:mw-quasi-parallelogram}. With \(P = 0\), it gives \(h(-Q) = h(Q) + O(1)\), so it suffices to prove the first part for \(n \geq 0\), by induction. It holds for \(n = 0\) and \(n = 1\). If it holds for \(n - 1\) and \(n\), then \eqref{eq:mw-quasi-parallelogram} for the pair \((nP, P)\) gives

\begin{equation*} \begin{aligned} h((n + 1)P) &= 2h(nP) + 2h(P) - h((n - 1)P) + O(1)\\ &= \bigl(2n^2 + 2 - (n - 1)^2\bigr) h(P) + O(1) = (n + 1)^2 h(P) + O(1). \end{aligned} \end{equation*}

For the second part, let \(c' \geq 0\) with \(h \geq -c'\), and \(C(Q) \coloneqq 2h(Q) + 2c' + c\), which is at least \(c \geq 0\). Then

\begin{equation*} h(P + Q) \leq 2h(P) + 2h(Q) - h(P - Q) + c \leq 2h(P) + 2h(Q) + c' + c \leq 2h(P) + C(Q). \end{equation*}

Theorem 8.4.5. Let \(h\) be a height on \(A\) with the Northcott property. Suppose that there are real numbers \(0 < a < b\) with the following properties.

  1. For every \(Q \in A\) there is a constant \(C_1(Q) \geq 0\) such that \(h(P + Q) \leq a\,h(P) + C_1(Q)\) for all \(P \in A\).
  2. There is a constant \(C_2 \geq 0\) such that \(h(mP) \geq b\,h(P) - C_2\) for all \(P \in A\).

If \(A/m\) is finite, then \(A\) is finitely generated. More precisely, let \(Q_1, \dots, Q_s\) represent the cosets of \(mA\) in \(A\), and \(C \coloneqq \max_i C_1(-Q_i) + C_2\), so that \(C \geq 0\). Then \(A\) is generated by \(Q_1, \dots, Q_s\) and the finitely many \(P \in A\) with \(h(P) \leq 1 + C/(b - a)\).

Proof.

Let \(P \in A\) and \(P_0 \coloneqq P\). For \(j \geq 1\), choose an index \(i_j\) and an element \(P_j \in A\) with \(P_{j-1} = mP_j + Q_{i_j}\). By the two hypotheses,

\begin{equation*} b\,h(P_j) \leq h(mP_j) + C_2 = h(P_{j-1} - Q_{i_j}) + C_2 \leq a\,h(P_{j-1}) + C. \end{equation*}

Let \(\rho \coloneqq a/b\), so \(0 < \rho < 1\). Then \(h(P_j) \leq \rho\,h(P_{j-1}) + C/b\), and, since \(\rho > 0\), induction on \(n\) gives

\begin{equation*} h(P_n) \leq \rho^n h(P) + \frac{C}{b}\bigl(1 + \rho + \dots + \rho^{n-1}\bigr) \leq \rho^n h(P) + \frac{C}{b - a}, \end{equation*}

where the last step uses \(C \geq 0\). Choose \(n\) with \(\rho^n h(P) \leq 1\). Then \(h(P_n) \leq 1 + C/(b - a)\), and \(P = m^n P_n + \sum_{j=1}^{n} m^{j-1} Q_{i_j}\). The set of \(P_n\) that can occur is finite by the Northcott property. This is the proof of [Sil09, Theorem VIII.3.1], which is the case \(a = 2\) and \(b = m^2\).

By Lemma 8.4.4, a quasi-quadratic height with the Northcott property satisfies the hypotheses of Theorem 8.4.5 with \(a = 2\) and \(b = m^2\), for every \(m \geq 2\). The case \(a = 1\) and \(b = m\) is the multiplicative group, in Remark 8.4.19.

Remark 8.4.6. The proof is effective given three inputs: representatives \(Q_1, \dots, Q_s\) of \(A/m\), the constants \(C_1(-Q_i)\) and \(C_2\), and the list of elements of \(A\) of bounded height [Sil09, Remark VIII.3.2]. For the heights on \(E(k)\), the constants and the list can be computed [Sil09, Exercise 8.18]. The representatives are the step that no known algorithm guarantees, as in Remark 8.2.9.

Example 8.4.7. No hypothesis of Theorem 8.4.5 can be dropped.

  1. Let \(A = \Q\), as an additive group, and \(h(x) \coloneqq \log \max(\lvert u \rvert, \lvert w \rvert)\) for \(x = u/w\) in lowest terms. It has the Northcott property, since there are at most \((2e^C + 1)^2\) such pairs \((u, w)\) with \(h(x) \leq C\). From \(u/w + u'/w' = (uw' + u'w)/(ww')\) we get \(h(x + y) \leq h(x) + h(y) + \log 2\), so part 1 of Theorem 8.4.5 holds with \(a = 1\), and \(\Q/m = 0\). But \(\Q\) is not finitely generated, since a finitely generated subgroup of \(\Q\) has bounded denominators. Here part 2 of Theorem 8.4.5 fails for every \(b > 1\): \(h(mx) \leq h(x) + \log m\).
  2. Let \(A = \bigoplus_{i \geq 1} \Z\) and \(h(x) \coloneqq \sum_i i\,x_i^2\). Each term satisfies the parallelogram law, so \(h\) is quadratic. It has the Northcott property: \(h(x) \leq C\) forces \(x_i = 0\) for \(i > C\) and \(\lvert x_i \rvert \leq \sqrt C\). But \(A/2\) is infinite, and \(A\) is not finitely generated.
  3. Conversely, let \(A\) be finitely generated, and choose an isomorphism \(A \cong A_\tors \times \Z^r\), with \(r \geq 0\). For \(P \in A\), let \(x_1(P), \dots, x_r(P) \in \Z\) be the coordinates of the image of \(P\) in \(\Z^r\), and \(h(P) \coloneqq x_1(P)^2 + \dots + x_r(P)^2\). Each \(x_i\) is a homomorphism \(A \to \Z\), so each \(x_i^2\) satisfies the parallelogram law, and \(h\) is quadratic. The set \(\{h \leq C\}\) is the preimage of finitely many elements of \(\Z^r\), and each preimage is a coset of the finite group \(A_\tors\). So \(h\) has the Northcott property.

So an abelian group \(A\) is finitely generated if and only if \(A/2\) is finite and \(A\) has a quasi-quadratic height with the Northcott property.

8.4.2 The canonical height

Lemma 8.4.8. Let \(q\) be a quadratic height on \(A\). Then \(q(0) = 0\), \(q(-P) = q(P)\), the pairing \(\langle\ ,\ \rangle_q\) is symmetric and bilinear, \(\langle P, P \rangle_q = 2q(P)\), and \(q(nP) = n^2 q(P)\) for every \(n \in \Z\).

Proof.

The parallelogram law with \(P = Q = 0\) gives \(2q(0) = 4q(0)\), and with \(P = 0\) it gives \(q(Q) + q(-Q) = 2q(Q)\). The pairing is symmetric by definition. Apply the parallelogram law to the pairs \((P + R, Q)\), \((P, R - Q)\), \((P + Q, R)\) and \((R, Q)\):

\begin{equation*} \begin{aligned} q(P + Q + R) + q(P + R - Q) &= 2q(P + R) + 2q(Q),\\ q(P + R - Q) + q(P + Q - R) &= 2q(P) + 2q(R - Q),\\ q(P + Q + R) + q(P + Q - R) &= 2q(P + Q) + 2q(R),\\ q(R + Q) + q(R - Q) &= 2q(R) + 2q(Q). \end{aligned} \end{equation*}

The first minus the second plus the third, minus twice the fourth, is twice

\begin{equation*} q(P + Q + R) - q(P + Q) - q(P + R) - q(Q + R) + q(P) + q(Q) + q(R) = 0, \end{equation*}

which says that \(\langle P + Q, R \rangle_q = \langle P, R \rangle_q + \langle Q, R \rangle_q\) [HS00, Lemma B.5.2]. The parallelogram law with \(Q = P\) gives \(q(2P) = 4q(P)\), so \(\langle P, P \rangle_q = q(2P) - 2q(P) = 2q(P)\). By bilinearity, \(q(nP) = \tfrac12 \langle nP, nP \rangle_q = n^2 q(P)\).

Proposition 8.4.9. Let \(h\) be a quasi-quadratic height on \(A\). For every \(P \in A\) the limit

\begin{equation*} \hat h(P) \coloneqq \lim_{n \to \infty} 4^{-n} h(2^n P) \end{equation*}

exists, and \(\hat h\) is the unique quadratic height on \(A\) with \(\hat h = h + O(1)\).

Proof.

By part 1 of Lemma 8.4.4, there is a constant \(c \geq 0\) with \(\lvert h(2P) - 4h(P) \rvert \leq c\) for all \(P\). Applied to \(2^n P\) and divided by \(4^{n+1}\), it gives

\begin{equation*} \bigl\lvert 4^{-(n+1)} h(2^{n+1} P) - 4^{-n} h(2^n P) \bigr\rvert \leq c\,4^{-(n+1)}. \end{equation*}

So the sequence is Cauchy, and summing from \(n = 0\) gives \(\lvert \hat h(P) - h(P) \rvert \leq c/3\). Let \(c' \geq 0\) bound the difference of the two sides of \eqref{eq:mw-quasi-parallelogram}. Applied to \(2^n P\) and \(2^n Q\) and divided by \(4^n\), the difference for \(4^{-n} h(2^n\,\cdot)\) is at most \(c'\,4^{-n}\), and tends to \(0\). So \(\hat h\) is quadratic. If \(q\) and \(q'\) are quadratic with \(q - q'\) bounded, then \(q - q'\) is quadratic, and \((q - q')(P) = n^{-2} (q - q')(nP)\) tends to \(0\) by Lemma 8.4.8. This is Tate's construction [Sil09, Proposition VIII.9.1 and Theorem VIII.9.3], [HS00, Theorem B.4.1].

We call \(\hat h\) the canonical height of \(h\). Equivalent quasi-quadratic heights have the same canonical height, by the uniqueness.

Corollary 8.4.10. Let \(h\) be a quasi-quadratic height on \(A\) with the Northcott property.

  1. The canonical height \(\hat h\) has the Northcott property, and \(\hat h(P) \geq 0\) for every \(P \in A\).
  2. We have \(\hat h(P) = 0\) if and only if \(P \in A_\tors\), and \(A_\tors\) is finite.
  3. For \(P \in A\) and \(T \in A_\tors\), \(\langle P, T \rangle_{\hat h} = 0\) and \(\hat h(P + T) = \hat h(P)\).
Proof.

The Northcott property passes to the equivalent height \(\hat h\), so \(\hat h\) is bounded below, say by \(-c\) with \(c \geq 0\). Then \(\hat h(P) = n^{-2} \hat h(nP) \geq -c\,n^{-2}\) for every \(n \geq 1\), so \(\hat h(P) \geq 0\). If \(nP = 0\) with \(n \geq 1\), then \(\hat h(P) = n^{-2} \hat h(0) = 0\). Conversely, if \(\hat h(P) = 0\), then \(\hat h(nP) = 0\) for every \(n\), so the multiples of \(P\) lie in the finite set \(\{\hat h \leq 0\}\), and \(P\) is torsion. That set contains \(A_\tors\). Finally, if \(nT = 0\) with \(n \geq 1\), then \(n \langle P, T \rangle_{\hat h} = \langle P, nT \rangle_{\hat h} = 0\), and \(\hat h(P + T) = \hat h(P) + \hat h(T) + \langle P, T \rangle_{\hat h} = \hat h(P)\).

8.4.3 Lattices and Minkowski's theorem

Let \(V\) be a real vector space of finite dimension \(r\), with a translation-invariant measure \(\vol\): in coordinates, a positive multiple of Lebesgue measure.

Definition 8.4.11. A lattice in \(V\) is a subgroup \(\Lambda\) generated by a basis \(\lambda_1, \dots, \lambda_r\) of \(V\). Its fundamental parallelepiped is \(F \coloneqq \{t_1 \lambda_1 + \dots + t_r \lambda_r : 0 \leq t_i < 1\}\), and its covolume is \(\covol(\Lambda) \coloneqq \vol(F)\).

The space \(V\) is the disjoint union of the translates \(F + \lambda\), for \(\lambda \in \Lambda\). Two bases of \(\Lambda\) differ by a matrix in \(\GL_r(\Z)\), of determinant \(\pm 1\), so the covolume does not depend on the basis.

Theorem 8.4.12 (Minkowski). Let \(\Lambda\) be a lattice in \(V\), and \(U \subseteq V\) a convex subset with \(-U = U\). If \(\vol(U) > 2^r \covol(\Lambda)\), then \(U\) contains a non-zero element of \(\Lambda\).

Proof.

Let \(U' \coloneqq \tfrac12 U\), so \(\vol(U') = 2^{-r} \vol(U) > \vol(F)\). The sets \(U' \cap (F + \lambda)\), for \(\lambda \in \Lambda\), partition \(U'\), and their translates \((U' \cap (F + \lambda)) - \lambda\) lie in \(F\). Their volumes add up to \(\vol(U') > \vol(F)\), so two of the translates meet: there are \(\lambda \neq \lambda'\) in \(\Lambda\) and \(x, x' \in U'\) with \(x - \lambda = x' - \lambda'\). Then \(2x\) and \(-2x'\) lie in \(U\), and so does their midpoint \(x - x' = \lambda - \lambda'\), a non-zero element of \(\Lambda\) [HS00, Theorem B.5.4].

Lemma 8.4.13. Let \(\Lambda\) be a lattice in \(V\), and \(q\) a quadratic form on \(V\). Suppose that \(q(\lambda) \neq 0\) for every non-zero \(\lambda \in \Lambda\), and that \(\{\lambda \in \Lambda : q(\lambda) \leq C\}\) is finite for every \(C\). Then \(q\) is positive definite.

Proof.

If \(q(\lambda) < 0\) for some \(\lambda \in \Lambda\), then the multiples \(n\lambda\) satisfy \(q(n\lambda) = n^2 q(\lambda) \leq 0\), and they are infinitely many. So \(q\) is positive on \(\Lambda \setminus \{0\}\), and \(\mu \coloneqq \min\{q(\lambda) : \lambda \in \Lambda, \lambda \neq 0\}\) exists, by the finiteness hypothesis, and is positive. Diagonalize \(q\): there are coordinates \(x_1, \dots, x_r\) on \(V\) in which \(q = x_1^2 + \dots + x_s^2 - x_{s+1}^2 - \dots - x_{s+t}^2\), with \(s + t \leq r\). Take \(\vol\) to be Lebesgue measure in these coordinates. Suppose that \(s < r\). For \(\delta > 0\), the set

\begin{equation*} U_\delta \coloneqq \Bigl\{x \in V : \sum_{i \leq s} x_i^2 \leq \frac{\mu}{2} \text{ and } \sum_{i > s} x_i^2 \leq \delta\Bigr\} \end{equation*}

is convex and symmetric, and its volume is a positive multiple of \(\delta^{(r-s)/2}\), which tends to infinity. By Theorem 8.4.12, for \(\delta\) large \(U_\delta\) contains a non-zero \(\lambda \in \Lambda\), and \(q(\lambda) \leq \sum_{i \leq s} x_i(\lambda)^2 \leq \mu/2\), a contradiction. So \(s = r\), and \(q\) is positive definite [Sil09, Lemma VIII.9.5].

Remark 8.4.14. The finiteness hypothesis in Lemma 8.4.13 cannot be dropped [Sil09, Remark VIII.9.4 and Exercise 8.16]. On \(V = \R^2\) with \(\Lambda = \Z^2\), the form \(q(x, y) \coloneqq (x - \sqrt 2\,y)^2\) is non-zero on \(\Lambda \setminus \{0\}\), since \(\sqrt 2\) is irrational, but it vanishes on the line \(x = \sqrt 2\,y\). The positivity of \(\hat h\) on \(A/A_\tors\), from Corollary 8.4.10, is the first hypothesis only. The point, due to Cassels, is that the second is needed to conclude that \(\hat h\) is positive definite on \(A_\R\).

8.4.4 Néron's counting theorem

Theorem 8.4.15. Let \(h\) be a quasi-quadratic height on \(A\) with the Northcott property, and suppose that \(A/m\) is finite.

  1. The group \(A\) is finitely generated, with \(A_\tors\) finite, so \(A \cong A_\tors \times \Z^r\) for some \(r \geq 0\).
  2. The image \(\Lambda\) of \(A\) in \(A_\R\) is a lattice, and the kernel of \(A \to \Lambda\) is \(A_\tors\).
  3. There is a unique quadratic form \(q\) on \(A_\R\) with \(\hat h(P) = q(P \otimes 1)\) for all \(P \in A\), and \(q\) is positive definite.
Proof.

The group \(A\) is finitely generated by Lemma 8.4.4 and Theorem 8.4.5, and \(A_\tors\) is finite. Since \(A_\tors \otimes \R = 0\), the group \(A_\R\) is \((A/A_\tors) \otimes \R \cong \R^r\), in which \(A/A_\tors \cong \Z^r\) is the standard lattice. By Lemma 8.4.8 and part 3 of Corollary 8.4.10, the pairing \(\langle\ ,\ \rangle_{\hat h}\) is a symmetric bilinear form on \(A/A_\tors\). It extends uniquely to a symmetric bilinear form \(B\) on \(A_\R\), and \(q(v) \coloneqq \tfrac12 B(v, v)\) satisfies \(q(P \otimes 1) = \tfrac12 \langle P, P \rangle_{\hat h} = \hat h(P)\). By Corollary 8.4.10, \(q\) is non-zero on \(\Lambda \setminus \{0\}\), and \(\{\lambda \in \Lambda : q(\lambda) \leq C\}\) is the image of the finite set \(\{\hat h \leq C\}\). So \(q\) is positive definite by Lemma 8.4.13 [Sil09, Proposition VIII.9.6].

Definition 8.4.16. In Theorem 8.4.15, let \(P_1, \dots, P_r \in A\) map to a basis of \(\Lambda\). The regulator of \(\hat h\) is \(\Reg(\hat h) \coloneqq \det\bigl(\langle P_i, P_j \rangle_{\hat h}\bigr)_{i,j}\).

Another choice replaces the Gram matrix \(G \coloneqq (\langle P_i, P_j \rangle_{\hat h})\) by \(U^{\mathsf T} G U\) with \(U \in \GL_r(\Z)\), so \(\Reg(\hat h)\) does not depend on the choice, and it is positive since \(q\) is positive definite. Let \(\omega_r \coloneqq \pi^{r/2}/\Gamma(r/2 + 1)\) be the volume of the unit ball in \(\R^r\).

Lemma 8.4.17. Let \(\Lambda\) be a lattice in \(V\), with \(r \geq 1\), and \(\lVert\ \rVert\) a norm on \(V\), with unit ball \(B \coloneqq \{v : \lVert v \rVert \leq 1\}\). Then, as \(T \to \infty\),

\begin{equation*} \#\{\lambda \in \Lambda : \lVert \lambda \rVert \leq T\} = \frac{\vol(B)}{\covol(\Lambda)}\, T^r + O(T^{r-1}). \end{equation*}
Proof.

Let \(F\) be a fundamental parallelepiped of \(\Lambda\), and \(D \coloneqq \sup_{y \in F} \lVert y \rVert\), finite since \(F\) is bounded. Let \(N(T)\) be the left side, and \(U_T\) the union of the \(F + \lambda\) with \(\lVert \lambda \rVert \leq T\). If \(\lVert x \rVert \leq T - D\), write \(x = y + \lambda\) with \(y \in F\). Then \(\lVert \lambda \rVert \leq \lVert x \rVert + \lVert y \rVert \leq T\), so \(x \in U_T\). If \(x = y + \lambda \in U_T\), then \(\lVert x \rVert \leq T + D\). So \((T - D)B \subseteq U_T \subseteq (T + D)B\), and taking volumes, for \(T \geq D\),

\begin{equation*} \vol(B)(T - D)^r \leq N(T) \covol(\Lambda) \leq \vol(B)(T + D)^r \end{equation*}

[HS00, Lemma B.6.4].

Theorem 8.4.18 (Néron). Let \(h\) be a quasi-quadratic height on \(A\) with the Northcott property, suppose that \(A/m\) is finite, and let \(r \geq 1\) be the rank of \(A\). Then, as \(T \to \infty\),

\begin{equation} \label{eq:mw-neron} \#\{P \in A : h(P) \leq T\} = \frac{\#A_\tors\, \omega_r\, 2^{r/2}}{\sqrt{\Reg(\hat h)}}\, T^{r/2} + O\bigl(T^{(r-1)/2}\bigr). \tag{8.4.2} \end{equation}
Proof.

By Proposition 8.4.9 there is a constant \(c \geq 0\) with \(\{\hat h \leq T - c\} \subseteq \{h \leq T\} \subseteq \{\hat h \leq T + c\}\). By Theorem 8.4.15, the map \(A \to \Lambda\) has fibers of size \(\#A_\tors\), and \(\hat h\) is the pullback of \(q\), so \(\#\{\hat h \leq T\} = \#A_\tors \cdot \#\{\lambda \in \Lambda : q(\lambda) \leq T\}\). Use the coordinates \(t \mapsto \sum_i t_i (P_i \otimes 1)\) on \(A_\R\), with Lebesgue measure, so that \(\covol(\Lambda) = 1\) and \(q(t) = \tfrac12 t^{\mathsf T} G t\). Apply Lemma 8.4.17 to the norm \(\sqrt q\), and \(\sqrt T\) in place of \(T\). The substitution \(t = \sqrt 2\, G^{-1/2} u\) maps the unit ball onto \(\{q \leq 1\}\), so \(\vol\{q \leq 1\} = \omega_r 2^{r/2}/\sqrt{\det G}\). Finally \((T \pm c)^{r/2} = T^{r/2} + O(T^{r/2 - 1})\), which is within the error term [HS00, Theorem B.6.3].

Let \(N(T)\) be the left side of \eqref{eq:mw-neron}. Then \(r = \lim_{T \to \infty} 2 \log N(T)/\log T\): the rank can be read off from the growth of the number of elements of bounded height. Hindry and Silverman state Theorem 8.4.18 for an abelian variety, with the multiplicative height \(e^h\), so that \(T^{r/2}\) becomes \((\log T)^{r/2}\), and for a canonical height that may have a linear part. A quasi-quadratic height has none, since \(h(-P) = h(P) + O(1)\).

8.4.5 The multiplicative group

Remark 8.4.19. Theorem 8.4.5 also applies to the multiplicative group, with a linear height in place of a quadratic one. Let \(k\) be a number field, \(S \subseteq \Places_k\) a finite set containing the archimedean places, and \(\calO_S^\times\) the group of \(S\)-units, as in Remark 8.3.5. In these notes \(\calO_S^\times\) is finitely generated by Dirichlet's \(S\)-unit theorem, which is only cited (Theorem 7.8.4), and the finiteness of \(\calO_S^\times/\calO_S^{\times m}\) is deduced from it (Proposition 7.8.3). An independent proof goes as follows. Enlarging \(S\) enlarges \(\calO_S^\times\), and a subgroup of a finitely generated abelian group is finitely generated, so we may assume that \(S\) contains the places \(v\) with \(v(m) > 0\).

  1. The group \(\calO_S^\times/\calO_S^{\times m}\) is finite. Let \(k' \coloneqq k(\mu_m)\), and \(S'\) the set of places of \(k'\) above \(S\). For \(x \in \calO_S^\times\), the extension \(k'(x^{1/m})/k'\) has degree at most \(m\) and is unramified outside \(S'\), by Proposition 7.7.4. By Hermite's theorem there are only finitely many such extensions [HS00, Proposition C.1.6], [Sil09, Remark VIII.1.7], so their compositum \(F\) is finite over \(k'\). By Corollary 7.4.6, the image of \(\calO_S^\times\) in \(k'^\times/k'^{\times m}\) embeds in the finite group \(\Hom(\Gal(F/k'), \mu_m(k'))\). The kernel of \(k^\times/k^{\times m} \to k'^\times/k'^{\times m}\) is finite, as in Lemma 8.2.2, and \(\calO_S^\times \cap k^{\times m} = \calO_S^{\times m}\), by valuations.
  2. For each \(v \in \Places_k\), let \(\lvert\ \rvert_v\) be the absolute value on \(k_v\) extending the usual one on \(\Q_p\) or \(\R\), and \(n_v \coloneqq [k_v : \Q_p]\) or \([k_v : \R]\). The Weil height of \(x \in k^\times\) is \(h(x) \coloneqq [k : \Q]^{-1} \sum_{v} n_v \log \max(1, \lvert x \rvert_v)\). It has the Northcott property on \(k\), viewed inside \(\PP^1(k)\) [HS00, Theorem B.2.3]. Since \(\max(1, \lvert xy \rvert_v) \leq \max(1, \lvert x \rvert_v) \max(1, \lvert y \rvert_v)\) and \(\max(1, \lvert x^m \rvert_v) = \max(1, \lvert x \rvert_v)^m\), it satisfies \(h(xy) \leq h(x) + h(y)\) and \(h(x^m) = m\,h(x)\).
  3. These are the hypotheses of Theorem 8.4.5 for \(\calO_S^\times\), written multiplicatively, with \(a = 1\), \(b = m\), \(C_1(y) = h(y)\) and \(C_2 = 0\). Here \(C_1(y) \geq 0\), since every term of \(h(y)\) is non-negative. So \(\calO_S^\times\) is finitely generated.

Hermite's theorem is itself proved with Minkowski's theorem [HS00, Theorem C.3.2], so this proof avoids Dirichlet's theorem but not the geometry of numbers. It does not give the rank, \(\#S - 1\) [HS00, Theorem C.3.3].

The analogue of Theorem 8.4.18 is a count of \(S\)-units. For \(x \in \calO_S^\times\), we have \(\lvert x \rvert_v = 1\) for \(v \notin S\), and the product formula \(\sum_{v \in S} n_v \log \lvert x \rvert_v = 0\) [HS00, Proposition B.1.2] gives

\begin{equation*} h(x) = \frac{1}{2[k : \Q]} \sum_{v \in S} n_v\, \bigl\lvert \log \lvert x \rvert_v \bigr\rvert. \end{equation*}

Under the logarithmic embedding \(x \mapsto (n_v \log \lvert x \rvert_v)_{v \in S}\), whose kernel is the finite group of roots of unity of \(k\) and whose image is a lattice in the hyperplane \(\sum_v y_v = 0\) by Dirichlet's theorem, \(h\) is a multiple of the \(\ell^1\) norm. It is a norm, not a quadratic form: \(h(x^n) = \lvert n \rvert h(x)\). So Lemma 8.4.17 applies to \(h\) itself, and, if \(\#S \geq 2\), the number of \(S\)-units with \(h(x) \leq T\) is \(c\,T^{\#S - 1} + O(T^{\#S - 2})\) for a constant \(c > 0\), with exponent the rank instead of half the rank. The constant \(c\) involves the covolume of the image, the \(S\)-regulator, in place of \(\Reg(\hat h)\).
